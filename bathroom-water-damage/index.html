Bathroom Water Damage in Springfield

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. In Springfield, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

Warning Signs

  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • A warped or swollen cabinet floor under the vanity sink
  • Caulk along the tub or shower that is cracked, peeling, or black with mold
  • Floor that feels spongy by the vanity or next to the tub
  • Edges of the vinyl floor peeling up, or dark discoloration showing through it
  • Dark grout that never seems to dry out between showers
Our Process

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Pinpoint the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First days

Water Slips Through

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

Months one to three

Signs Start to Appear

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Rot Under the Toilet

Each flush leaks a small amount once the wax ring fails. The toilet eventually rocks because this category 3 water has rotted the subfloor at the flange.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. The cabinet floor swells, and the water runs through to the subfloor and the wall behind it.

Damage Downstairs

Water follows pipes and joists before it drips, so the stain downstairs may sit several feet from the actual leak. Light fixtures and wiring can be in its path.

Mold in the Tiled Wall

By themselves, grout and tile do not keep water out. When the layer behind them stays wet, mold grows on the wall board where cleaning never reaches.
