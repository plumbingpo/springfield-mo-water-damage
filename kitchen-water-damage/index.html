Kitchen Water Damage: What Springfield Homeowners Should Know

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. Our Springfield crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

Warning Signs

  • Toe kicks that have swollen, darkened, or pulled away from the cabinet base
  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking
  • Stale, damp air that hits you when the doors under the sink are opened
  • Cupped or raised floorboards, wood or laminate, near the dishwasher door
  • A brown stain on the ceiling of the room below the kitchen

Our Process

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Shut Off the Leak

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Map the Moisture

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry Without Demolition

With the toe kicks off, air gets pushed below the cabinets and behind them. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Remove Only Damage

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

The Damage Timeline

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Begin to Swell

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

Past one week

Tear Out Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. A leak there wets drywall and framing that the cabinets cover completely, so mold can spread across the wall unseen.

Toe Kick Space

Base cabinets sit over a gap that is boxed in all around. Water collects there, stays for weeks, and grows mold that nobody sees until the cabinets are pulled.

Layers of Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Between those layers water is trapped, unable to evaporate and invisible from above.

The Ice Maker Line

An ice maker is fed by a thin tube that may lose a few drops each minute. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.
