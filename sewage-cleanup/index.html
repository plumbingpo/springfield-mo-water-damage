Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. Across Springfield, Springfield Restoration Squad answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
