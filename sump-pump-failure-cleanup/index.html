Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Springfield

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. Water rises over the rim of the pit and spreads across the basement floor. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. Having passed through the ground, it usually holds bacteria, silt and drain tile residue, which makes it Category 2 at best, or Category 3 once it has been standing or sewage has mixed in. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stay Out of the WaterNever wade into basement water before the power has been cut. Water may be touching outlets, the furnace or the pump's own cord.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. Call the utility when the panel itself sits in the flooded area.
  3. Check the Pump SafelySee if the plug pulled out, the outlet has tripped or the float got stuck on the side of the pit. Keep your hands out of the water.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Move Valuables and CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. To keep the claim file complete, our team documents the damage, the failed pump and the height of the water.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Dirt and small rocks washed into the basin that lock up the pump.
  • Outside the house, a crushed, clogged or separated discharge pipe.
  • A pump too small for the volume of water entering the pit.
  • Check valve trouble, which makes the pump move the same water again and again.
  • A power outage during the storm with no battery backup on the pump.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Furnace and Water Heater

The furnace and water heater frequently sit on the same floor as the pit. If wiring, burners or control boards got wet they may be ruined, and no appliance should be restarted until it has been checked.

Carpet and Flooring

There is no safe way to clean pad and carpet that sat in groundwater, so both are removed. Silty water also gets caught below vinyl plank and laminate, which normally means lifting them.

Outlets and Wiring

Rising water can get into receptacles mounted low on basement walls. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.

Boxes and Stored Items

Cardboard boxes, furniture legs, books and fabrics soak up contaminated water quickly. Within days mold shows up on absorbent things that were in it, and they are difficult to disinfect.
